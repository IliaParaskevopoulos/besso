Who buys Besso: the ideal customer, the people who decide, and how to reach them

Built from Besso’s website, its funding coverage, job posts at target companies and competitor material. Evidence tags like S1 point to the sources at the bottom; Hypothesis marks anything still to validate in customer interviews.

Assumptions: the product profiled is the full platform (savings finder + regulation monitoring). Europe-headquartered multinationals are the core segment; the US push after the seed round is treated as a second segment to validate.

The three ICPs

The same three segments as on the value propositions page. ICP 1 is the beachhead and ICP 2 the next segment; both are profiled in depth below. ICP 3 is a growth bet until there is a lighter offer.

ICP 1 · Beachhead

Global food & beverage majors

Multinational food, drink and consumer-goods groups moving ingredients, packaging and finished goods through dozens of trade lanes.

Why now

Tariff shocks, cost-savings programmes, new trade agreements in force.

Public customers

Danone, Unilever, AB InBev, Coca-Cola, Heineken, Philip Morris International

Value proposition

For global food and beverage groups who overpay duty across dozens of trade lanes, besso finds the savings hidden in tariff schedules and trade agreements, and keeps watching more than 1,250 rules after a broker or consultant has checked a lane once.

Chemicals & agri-science groups

Global chemical, crop-protection and seed companies with complex product codes and multi-stage production across countries.

For chemical and agri-science groups whose products cross borders several times before they are sold, besso shows where trade agreements still save duty after every production change, pairing AI monitoring with legal research.

1 · Ideal customer profile (ICP 1 and 2 in depth)

The ideal customer in one line

A Europe- or US-headquartered food, beverage, tobacco, chemicals or crop-science manufacturer that ships its own bills of materials across three or more trade-agreement corridors, has an in-house customs team working in SAP GTS, and still qualifies preferential origin in spreadsheets or through consultants.

Firmographics

Industry and sub-segment

Branded FMCG food and beverage (beer, soft drinks, ice cream, dairy), tobacco, specialty and agro chemicals, crop science. Adjacent: ingredients, consumer health, precious metals. S1 S4

Company size

Core: 10,000+ employees, revenue above €5B (Unilever, BASF, AB InBev, Syngenta tier). Second tier: importers of €300M–2B, where Besso puts missed preference at $2–15M a year. S1 S4 Hypothesis: revenue bands

Region and language

HQ in Switzerland, Germany, Benelux, UK, Nordics; then US. English for the platform; German and French for Swiss and DACH meetings. Corridors EU–CH–UK–US–Mexico/Canada–Asia. S5 S7

Business model and maturity

Own production plants in several countries, intercompany flows, frequent sourcing changes. Mature, listed or family-owned, with a group cost-savings programme. S2

Technographics

SAP S/4HANA with SAP GTS (or AEB, MIC); customs brokers; Excel for origin calculations; may already pay Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE or Descartes. S7 S8 S10 S11 S12

Signals: they need it now

  • A new trade agreement hits a corridor they use: EU–India and EU–Indonesia expected in 2027 S15
  • US tariff changes or the USMCA six-year review (from July 2026) S14
  • Hiring a Head of Customs or Trade Compliance with “preferential origin / FTA” in the post S7 S8
  • SAP GTS rollout or S/4HANA migration under way S9
  • Group cost-out target where supplier savings are exhausted S2
  • Customs audit, or an origin verification request from an authority S13

Disqualifiers: looks like a fit, isn’t

  • Mostly domestic sales, or one corridor only
  • No in-house customs owner: everything sits with the broker
  • Just signed a multi-year FTA-management roll-out (Descartes FTI, ONESOURCE FTA) S11 S12
  • Services, software or pure distributors with no bill of materials to qualify Hypothesis

Score each 0–3, multiply by the weight, total out of 300. A-lead: 210+. B: 150–209. Below 150: nurture.

4 · Persona cards

Champion

Global Head of Customs & Trade Compliance, food / chemicals multinational

Owns classification, valuation and preferential origin; works in SAP GTS; reports into Supply Chain or Group Tax.

Opening line

“When EU–India comes into force next year, who on your team will requalify the bills of materials — and how much of it will be done in Excel?”

Goals, in their words

  • “Defensible positions” on origin in every audit
  • Claim every preference we are entitled to
  • Run customs as a business service, not a cost centre
  • Get the team out of spreadsheets and PDFs

Pains

  • Origin calculations and supplier declarations kept by hand in spreadsheets and PDFs S2 S4
  • Rule changes come faster than the team can read them (Besso counts 7,000+ a week) S1
  • Preference left unclaimed because the team doesn’t trust its origin data or audit file S13
  • Each new agreement means another consultant project S2
  • SAP GTS runs the process but doesn’t show where money is left on the table S20 · hypothesis

Triggers

  • A new trade agreement on one of their lanes (EU–India, EU–Indonesia, 2027)
  • Customs audit or origin verification request
  • SAP GTS roll-out or S/4HANA migration
  • Newly hired into the role

Objections and replies

“An AI can’t sign an origin statement.”

It doesn’t. Each finding shows the rule and the data used; your team decides and signs.

“We already have SAP GTS.”

GTS runs the process you configured. Besso reads the same data and shows which agreements, routes and shipments you aren’t using.

“My team has no time for another project.”

Start with a light data sample, no migration. Besso says customers free up to 25% of team capacity — ask for that on your sample.

Decision criteria

Rule coverage for our corridorsTrace to legal textFits SAP GTSTime to first findingWho keeps rules current

Where to find them

ICPA European Conference (London, 7–8 Apr 2027); Customs Compliance in Europe Conference (Düsseldorf, Nov); KPMG EMEA Trade & Customs Conference; PwC Switzerland customs law day; LinkedIn search: “Head of Customs”, “Global Trade Compliance”, “preferential origin”.

Decision maker

VP / Chief Supply Chain Officer, consumer goods or chemicals group

Owns sourcing, plants and logistics cost; carries the group cost-out target. Besso’s published testimonial comes from this title.

Opening line

“Your teams have worked suppliers and freight hard; duty is often the line nobody has fully worked. We can show what your top corridors leave on the table, from one data sample.”

Goals, in their words

  • Hit the cost-out target without squeezing suppliers again
  • Sourcing and route decisions with duty in the model
  • Fewer surprises when tariffs change

Pains

  • Usual savings levers are used up — Besso’s buyers say they have “exhausted” them S2
  • Tariff and trade politics make sourcing plans go stale S2
  • Duty cost is spread across units and hard to see as one number hypothesis
  • Advisory projects end in a report, not ongoing savings S2

Triggers

  • Annual budget and cost-out targets
  • Tariff shock on a key lane (US tariffs, USMCA review)
  • Plant move or new supplier country
  • Board asks about tariff exposure

Objections and replies

“Vendor savings numbers are always inflated.”

Ask for the figure on your own data, checked by your customs team before any contract.

“A Bern startup for a global process?”

Customers include Unilever, BASF and AB InBev; CHF 4M seed in Oct 2026; advisory board with former State Secretary Jean-Daniel Gerber.

“Tariffs are policy, not supply chain.”

Origin and routing are supply chain choices. Besso simulates their duty impact before you decide.

Decision criteria

Size of verified savingTime to first savingLoad on my teamsVendor stability

Gartner Supply Chain Symposium (hypothesis); SupplyChainBrain and Supply Chain Dive; Big Four supply chain partners; peer referrals from existing Besso accounts; LinkedIn Sales Navigator by title in target accounts.

Financial approver

Head of Group Tax (indirect tax & customs) or CFO office

At many multinationals customs sits in tax or corporate finance — Bühler posts its customs roles under Corporate Finance.

Opening line

“Duty is one of few costs you can cut without a supplier negotiation. Want to see how much of it you are entitled not to pay?”

Goals, in their words

  • Turn duty savings into booked cash
  • No penalty exposure from wrong preference claims
  • Spend less on recurring advisory fees

Pains

  • Higher tariffs eat straight into gross margin S22
  • Authorities look harder at origin claims and records S13
  • Savings potential and booked savings rarely reconciled hypothesis
  • Repeat Big Four fees for each agreement or audit hypothesis

Triggers

  • Budget cycle
  • Tariff cost visible in quarterly margin
  • Customs audit finding

Objections and replies

“Is this booked money or a potential?”

Besso’s dashboard separates savings potential from recovered year to date. Agree the measure before you start.

“The budget is set.”

Ask Besso whether a pilot can be scoped against savings found (to validate). Besso claims 10–15× average ROI.

“Who is liable if a claim is rejected?”

Your team keeps the decision and the audit trail. Contract terms to confirm with Besso.

ROI on own dataRisk reductionCost vs consultantsContract flexibility

KPMG EMEA Trade & Customs Conference; PwC Switzerland customs law day; Big Four indirect-tax partners; Customs Compliance in Europe Conference (general counsels, customs directors attend).

SAP GTS / ERP owner with InfoSec, manufacturing group

Runs SAP GTS master data and broker interfaces; signs off any new SaaS that touches trade data.

  • Clean master data, nothing breaks in GTS
  • Pass security review without exceptions

Pains

  • GTS roll-outs and S/4 changes already fill the roadmap S9

Start with a light sample; upload or connect; no full migration.

“Which connectors? Where is data hosted?”

Besso’s site names no ERP connector or hosting detail. Close this gap with a security pack before evaluation (to validate).

“Another tool to maintain.”

Besso keeps its 1,250+ tracked regulations current, so no one maintains rule tables in-house.

Decision criteria
